# LC3 evidence → next model | Group discussion

## 1 · Research

I organized the available LC3 evidence into seven reproducible research modules. Each module records findings, source references, and figures **where the data support a useful comparison**.

| Module | What to show | Takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| `lc3/01_manufacturer_projects/` | Project locations / announcements | Announced capacity is not necessarily operating supply. |
| `lc3/02_market_demand/` | Demand evidence | Demand potential is not observed LC3 purchasing. |
| `lc3/03_regulations_standards/` | Documented state research / testing | Research and general cement eligibility are not LC3 approval. |
| `lc3/04_manufacturer_economics/` | Calcined-clay costs; sourcing/payback | Investment viability varies with local economics; published cases are not U.S. plant observations. |
| `lc3/05_rmx_adoption_decisions/` | LC3 concrete demonstration | Technical feasibility does not establish commercial adoption decisions. |
| `lc3/06_external_signals/` | Procurement / policy evidence | Low-carbon incentives are not LC3-specific demand. |
| `lc3/07_evidence_synthesis/` | Evidence-to-model mapping | Separates documented findings from unresolved assumptions. |

## 2 · What this tells us

- We have evidence on **potential supply, economics, standards, technical performance, and policy signals**.
- We have much less evidence on **actual LC3 manufacturer/RMX adoption decisions, national adoption rates, and acceptance dates**.
- Therefore: **use the evidence to design and challenge mechanisms; do not turn missing observations into invented initial dates or calibrated LC3 parameters.**

## 3 · The original three questions

1. **Reproduce PLC:** Can the model match observed historical diffusion, including geographic patterns?
2. **Explain PLC:** Which mechanisms are actually needed to account for that diffusion?
3. **Explore LC3:** Under what conditions could LC3 adoption accelerate, and what coordination helps?

## 4 · A possible simpler next model

**Keep:** Geographic manufacturer–RMX network and distinct manufacturer, RMX, and DOT roles.

**Test:** Can **manufacturer investment timing** emerge from expected demand and economics, rather than a preset deployment date? Can **regulatory decisions** respond to evidence rather than a fixed acceptance schedule? Distinguish *decision timing* from unavoidable physical construction/testing durations.

**Start with few explicit rules:**

- **Manufacturer:** Invest if anticipated demand makes investment viable.
- **RMX:** Adopt if supply is accessible, the material is eligible for its intended use, and adoption is commercially worthwhile.
- **DOT:** Consider acceptance as technical evidence accumulates; do not equate research milestones with approvals.

These are **candidate rules, not validated descriptions of all actors**. Add learning, peer effects, or AI-driven decisions only if they answer a question the simpler model cannot.

## 5 · What could we test?

| Hypothesis | Comparison |
|---|---|
| **Coordination bottleneck** | Independent decisions vs credible demand commitments / coordinated investment |
| **Supply–regulation complementarity** | Regulatory change alone vs supply expansion alone vs both |
| **Regional lock-in** | Different initial locations of economically viable manufacturer investment |

**Success is not just a better adoption curve:** Look for robust, interpretable mechanisms, geographic differences, and clear counterfactuals.
